# Community guidelines

buildwith.uz works when people come here to learn and help each other. These guidelines borrow from Hacker News and Reddit and adapt them to a small Uzbek community. They are not a legal code: moderators use judgement, and the spirit matters more than the letter.

## What belongs here

Anything a good engineer in Uzbekistan would find interesting: programming, infrastructure, data, design, hardware, building products, open source, and careers in tech here and abroad. Local tech news, hiring and the realities of working from Tashkent or Samarkand are welcome.

A rule of thumb for off-topic posts: if it would be just as good on a general news site and it has nothing to teach an engineer, it probably belongs elsewhere. Politics, celebrity news and memes without substance usually do.

## Be kind

- Criticise ideas, not people. “This query will lock the table” helps; “you clearly don't know SQL” does not.
- Assume good faith. Read the most reasonable version of what someone said before you reply.
- Don't post flamebait, and don't take the bait. If a thread turns into a fight, step away.
- No slurs, no insults about anyone's nationality, region, language, religion, gender or age.
- No harassment, no threats, and no publishing anyone's private information (doxxing).
- Newcomers ask simple questions. Everyone was one once; answer kindly or let someone else answer.

## Posting

- **Link to the original source**, not a repost or a summary of it.
- **Use the original title.** Don't add your opinion to it and don't write clickbait. If the original title is misleading or pure bait, use a plain, accurate one.
- **Questions go in Savol.** Use the Savol type so answers can be voted and the best one rises to the top.
- **Pick tags that fit** — up to three — so people who follow a topic can find your post.

### Koʻrsat rules

Koʻrsat is for something you made that people can try, run or see: an app, a library, a tool, a game, a piece of hardware.

- It must be your own work, or work you took a real part in.
- People must be able to try it. A landing page, a sign-up wall, a waitlist or a sales pitch is not a Koʻrsat.
- Say what it is, why you built it and what you want feedback on.
- Be there to answer questions in the comments.

### Self-promotion

Sharing your own work is fine, in moderation. A good rule of thumb: most of what you post should not be yours. Posts from the same domain over and over are ranked lower automatically, and accounts that only promote themselves may be limited.

## Savol etiquette

When you ask:

1. Search first. Your question may already have a good answer.
2. Say what you are trying to do, and what you have already tried.
3. Include the exact error text, the versions you use and a short piece of code that shows the problem.
4. When an answer helps you, upvote it and say so, so the next reader knows it works.

When you answer:

1. Answer the question that was asked. If you think the approach is wrong, explain why, then still help.
2. Show how you know: a link to the documentation, a snippet or your own experience.
3. Don't post “same problem” replies; upvote the question instead.

## Voting and karma

Upvote what is useful, interesting or well made, even if you disagree with it. Votes are not likes for your friends: voting rings, asking for votes and using several accounts are not allowed and will be removed.

Karma is the sum of the votes your posts, articles and comments have received. Some actions open up as your karma grows:

- **30 karma:** you can flag posts and comments.
- **100 karma:** you can downvote comments.
- Links posted by accounts with less than **50 karma** are marked `nofollow`, so the site is not worth spamming for search engines.

## New accounts

For the first 7 days, an account can post up to 2 posts, 20 notes, 1 article and 10 comments a day. This keeps spam out and costs real people very little. The first link to a domain we have not seen before may wait for a moderator before it appears.

## Flagging and reporting

Flag a post or comment when it breaks these guidelines, not because you disagree with it. Posts with several flags from trusted members are ranked lower, and heavily flagged ones are hidden until a moderator looks.

Every post, note, article, comment and profile has a **Report** button. Use it for spam, abuse, illegal content or anything that needs a moderator's attention. Reports are private.

## Moderation

Moderators are volunteers from the community and are listed by name. Every action they take — hiding, restoring, renaming, featuring, banning — is recorded in the public [moderation log](/modlog) with a reason.

If you think a decision was wrong, ask in GitHub discussions or use the report form on the item, calmly and with details. Moderators can make mistakes and will reverse them.

## Mute and block

You don't have to read everyone. **Mute** hides a person's posts from you without them knowing. **Block** also stops them from replying to you, following you or mentioning you. You can also mute words. Mutes and blocks apply everywhere on the site.
